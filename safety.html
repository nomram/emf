Safety & scope

This is a guide to understanding waves, not to building transmitters or weapons. Reading is safe. The real world has a few sharp edges, listed here.

What this guide covers, and what it leaves out

We explain the physics and the engineering behind antennas, radar, EMPs and the microwave oven. We do not give build instructions for jammers, EMP devices, high-power microwave sources or anything meant to damage other people’s equipment. Several chapters use real numbers (power, frequency, shielding) because understanding needs them, not because the guide wants you to reproduce a device.

Radio and microwave exposure

  • Radio waves below the ultraviolet are non-ionising: a single photon has too little energy to break chemical bonds. The risk is heating, which depends on power density and frequency. Chapter 5 shows the numbers.
  • Do not stand in front of a radar, a high-power transmitter or the feed of a dish while it is on. Public exposure limits exist (ICNIRP, IEEE C95.1) and are typically a few W/m² to tens of W/m² in the microwave range.
  • Transmitting on most frequencies needs a licence. Unlicensed bands (Wi-Fi, ISM, some low-power devices) have strict power limits.

Microwave ovens and mains

Never open a microwave ovenThe high-voltage capacitor behind the cover can hold a lethal charge after unplugging. Chapter 17 explains how an oven works so that you do not need to open one.

Law and ethics

Deliberately interfering with radio communications (jamming), GPS or aviation is illegal almost everywhere and can endanger lives. Treat the interference examples in this guide as explanations of why a problem happens and how to protect against it.
